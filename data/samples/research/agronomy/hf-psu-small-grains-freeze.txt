Title: Will Late Spring Low Temperatures Affect Small Grains?
Author/publisher: Heidi Reed (and co-authors listed on page), Penn State Extension
Year: page updated April 7, 2026
URL: https://extension.psu.edu/will-late-spring-low-temperatures-affect-small-grains
Date opened: 14 Nov 2026
Locator: paragraph on general crop cold hardiness; Table 1 "Freeze Injury in Wheat" (adapted from University of Kentucky)

Quoted text:
"we can generalize that the average cereal rye is cold hardier than all other small grains. Next in cold hardiness is triticale, followed by wheat, then barley, which is most susceptible."
"At Feekes stage 10.5, or flowering (anthers visible), temperatures need only reach 30 degrees for 2 hours for injury to occur."
Table 1 (wheat, approximate injurious temp, 2 hours): Tillering (1-5) 12°F; Jointing (6-7) 24°F; Boot (10) 28°F; Heading (10.1-.5) 30°F; Flowering (10.51-.54) 30°F; Milk (11.1) 28°F; Dough (11.2) 28°F.
Note: no barley- or rye-specific temperatures are given; only the ranking above.
