Authors: Steduto, P., Hsiao, T.C., Fereres, E. & Raes, D. (eds.)
Publisher/year: FAO, Rome, 2012

Opened: 14 Nov 2026 (curl + PyMuPDF text extraction). Book page = PDF page - 15.

--- Barley chapter, "Growth and Development", book page 136 (PDF 151) ---
"Winter barley is sown in autumn and requires a cold period (vernalization) during early growth for
flowering under long days ... This ensures flowering when the risk of late frost is low"

--- Barley chapter, book page 137 (PDF 152) ---
"The base temperature and upper temperature for barley should be similar to that of wheat, 0 oC and
around 25 oC, respectively."

--- Barley chapter, book page 139 (PDF 154) ---
"In most temperate conditions grain filling occurs when the weather is getting hotter. High temperatures
(above 30-32 ºC) accelerate seed development towards maturity, shortening the grain filling period and
hence reduces weight per grain at harvest. The effect is noticeable even if periods of high temperature
are short (even only 3 days)."

--- Barley chapter, "Yield", book page 140 (PDF 155) ---
"barley yield is more sensitive to changes in growing conditions during the period when grain number is
set, from stem elongation to just before the onset of grain filling. The cumulative growth during this
phase is important enough to justify the assumption of some risk of frost at flowering"

--- Soybean chapter, book page 128 (PDF 143) ---
"Soybean is very sensitive to frost at seed emergence and pod filling"

--- Maize chapter, book page 119 (PDF 134) ---
"Being a C4 and warm-season crop, maize is sensitive to cold. In AquaCrop ... the base temperature
(Tbase) for maize is set at 8 °C, and the upper temperature threshold (Tupper) ... is set at 30 °C."
(Development-rate limits, not damage thresholds.)

--- Potato chapter, book page 187 (PDF 202) ---
"The temperature for frost or cold stress for potato is considered 1 oC and less, whereas that of heat
stress is 35 oC or higher."

--- Sugar beet chapter, book page 202 (PDF 217) ---
"Planting should be done when soil temperature is higher than 4 °C and the risks of frost is nil"
(No damaging air temperature is given.)

--- Alfalfa chapter, book page 215 (PDF 230) ---
"After floral initiation, temperature alone drives development through flowering, seed filling and
maturity. Temperatures above 27 °C, water stress or excessive soil moisture conditions can decrease seed
yields."  (Seed yield, not hay; not usable for a hay crop.)
--- Alfalfa chapter, book page 217 (PDF 232) ---
"At temperatures less than 5 °C or greater than 45 °C, alfalfa development is negligible."
--- Alfalfa chapter, book page 218 (PDF 233) ---
"Stands can persist for over 20 years in low rainfall climates that have a distinct winter dormant
period, provided soils do not freeze and cause plant death."
